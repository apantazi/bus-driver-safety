Critical Errors — TSV Is Flat-Out Wrong (1 state)

Tennessee Inaccurate
  • Critical Transit/bus-driver-specific treatment & "Distinct from violence in general?"
    TSV claims: "No current public-transit-driver-specific assault enhancement located; historical transit-employee aggravated-assault language appears to have been deleted." Distinct column: "No current distinct transit category located."
    What the law actually says: Tennessee Code Annotated § 39-13-102(d) still exists and explicitly covers transit employees. It provides: "A person commits aggravated assault who, with intent to cause physical injury to any public employee or an employee of a transportation system, public or private, whose operation is authorized by title 7, chapter 56, causes physical injury to the employee while the public employee is performing a duty within the scope of the public employee's employment or while the transportation system employee is performing an assigned duty on, or directly related to, the operation of a transit vehicle." This is a Class A misdemeanor (up to 11 months 29 days in jail and/or fine up to $2,500). The TSV's claim that this language was deleted is incorrect. The cached files for Tennessee were Cloudflare-blocked, which likely led to this error.
Recommended correction: Tennessee should be changed from "No" to "Yes" in the transit-specific treatment and "Distinct from violence in general?" columns. The statute number should add TCA § 39-13-102(d). The covered worker/setting should note "employee of a transportation system, public or private, whose operation is authorized by TCA title 7, ch. 56." The felony/misdemeanor column should note Class A misdemeanor. The punishment column should note up to 11 months 29 days and/or up to $2,500 fine.
Cache integrity issue: Both Tennessee source files were Cloudflare-blocked with no usable text, preventing verification during original research. The transit-employee provision was confirmed by independent web search against Justia, FindLaw, and TNDocket sources.

Needs Significant Nuance — Material Omissions or Imprecision (5 states)

Louisiana Needs Nuance
  • Moderate Covered worker/setting — "train operator" claim unsupported
    TSV claims: "Bus operator or train operator while operating a bus/train" and "Statute covers battery committed without consent when the offender has reasonable grounds to believe the victim is a bus operator or train operator operating a bus or train."
    What the law actually says: La. R.S. 14:34.5.1 is titled Battery of a bus operator. The definition of "bus operator" covers persons employed by a public transit system who operate a bus, or who operate an electronically operated cable car. The statute does not mention "train operator" in its title or definition. The title of this statute has been consistently "Battery of a bus operator" across 2011, 2015, and 2018 editions. The most recent amendment (Acts 2024, No. 367, §1) does not appear to have added train operators based on available sources. The TSV's "bus operator or train operator" framing overstates coverage.
  • Moderate Cache integrity — wrong statute cached
    TSV source file: la_14_34_5_1.html
    What was actually cached: The file la_14_34_5_1.html captured RS 14:224 (Transportation of water from St. Tammany Parish prohibited) — a completely unrelated statute. No verification of RS 14:34.5.1 is possible from the cached file.
Recommended correction: Change "bus operator or train operator" to "bus operator (or electronically operated cable car operator)." Verify whether the 2024 amendment added any additional covered workers. Re-cache the correct statute page at legis.la.gov/Legis/Law.aspx?d=206144.
Arizona Needs Nuance
  • Moderate Missing protections — same 2025 bill added airport employees and railway workers
    TSV claims: Only transit employees in buses/vans/shuttles mentioned. Statute listed as only A.R.S. § 13-1204(A)(8)(k).
    What the law actually says: 2025 Ariz. Sess. Laws ch. 172 added three new protected categories in the same bill under A.R.S. § 13-1204(A)(8): (k) public transit employees performing duties on/off a bus, van, or shuttle; and also (l) airport employees, and (m) railway workers (in the pre-2033 version). The TSV mentions only the transit employee provision and omits the airport and railway additions from the same bill.
  • Moderate Dual-version structure not noted — subsection letters change after 2032
    TSV claims: "A.R.S. section 13-1204(A)(8)(k), as amended by 2025 Ariz. Sess. Laws ch. 172" (single citation)
    What the law actually says: Ch. 172 creates two parallel versions of A.R.S. § 13-1204 because of a Proposition 311 sunset provision. Section 1 (operative until January 1, 2033) places transit employees at (A)(8)(k). Section 2 (operative after December 31, 2032) places them at (A)(8)(l) — one letter later — because of differing numbering in the post-sunset version. A citation to only "(A)(8)(k)" is incomplete.
  • Minor Paraphrase of covered worker scope slightly altered
    TSV claims: "performing duties on or off a bus, van, or shuttle"
    What the statute says: "performs duties on and off a vehicle while engaged in transferring members of the community to and from destinations in a bus, van or shuttle." The coverage is for duties on and off the vehicle (not "on or off a bus"), while the employee is engaged in transit work. The paraphrase inverts the qualifying phrase.
Missing from TSV
  • Airport employees: A.R.S. § 13-1204(A)(8)(l) [pre-2033] / (m) [post-2032] — added by same ch. 172
  • Railway workers: A.R.S. § 13-1204(A)(8)(m) [pre-2033] / (n) [post-2032] — added by same ch. 172
  • New paragraph 12: aided by two or more accomplices causing physical injury = Class 4 felony (also added by ch. 172)
Illinois Needs Nuance
  • Moderate 625 ILCS 50/1 citation — notice/posting act, not a criminal penalty provision
    TSV claims: Statute numbers include "625 ILCS 50/1" in the same list as 720 ILCS 5/12-2 and 5/12-3.05. The description says "Posting law states assault against a transit employee is Class A misdemeanor and physical harm is Class 3 felony."
    What the law actually says: 625 ILCS 50 is the Public Conveyance Notice Act — a law that requires transit agencies to post notices about the penalties for assaulting transit employees. It is a signage/administrative requirement, not a criminal statute itself. The criminal penalties (Class A misdemeanor for aggravated assault, Class 3 felony for aggravated battery against transportation personnel) come from 720 ILCS 5/12-2 and 720 ILCS 5/12-3.05 respectively. Listing 625 ILCS 50/1 under "Statute number(s)" in a criminal-law table is misleading. The TSV note does acknowledge it is a "notice/signage requirement," which mitigates but doesn't fully resolve the confusion from listing it alongside criminal statutes.
Recommended correction: Move 625 ILCS 50/1 to the Notes column only. The "Statute number(s)" column should list only the criminal provisions: 720 ILCS 5/12-2 and 5/12-3.05.

Maryland Mostly Accurate
  • Minor Section 7-705(e) lower fine tier not mentioned
    TSV only describes: Section 7-705(f) interference penalty (fine up to $1,000, imprisonment up to 1 year).
    What the cached file shows: Section 7-705(e) provides a separate, lower penalty for general transit-rule violations (fine not more than $500) distinct from the obstruct/hinder/interfere paragraph in (f) which carries the higher $1,000/1-year penalty. This distinction matters for practitioners.
Both Maryland statutes confirmed accurate from cached files: 7-705(f) interference penalty ($1,000 / 1 year) verified; 7-705.1 transit-ban process (administrative, not criminal) verified. The TSV's characterization that 7-705.1 is not a criminal enhancement is confirmed correct.
Minnesota Mostly Accurate
  • Minor Section 609.855 subd. 5 — shooting at transit vehicles not mentioned
    TSV covers: Section 609.855 interference with transit operator (up to 3 years/$5,000 for force-based interference; up to 90 days/$1,000 otherwise).
    What the cached file also shows: Minn. Stat. § 609.855 subd. 5 separately covers shooting at a transit vehicle — up to 3 years/$6,000 if unoccupied; up to 5 years/$10,000 if occupied. This is an additional protection not noted in the TSV.
  • Minor 2024 revisor instruction to section 609.2231 subd. 11 not noted
    TSV does not note any 2024 change.
    What the cached file shows: The transit operator assault provision in § 609.2231 subd. 11 received a revisor's instruction in 2024 (2024 c 104 art 2 s 15) that may have updated cross-references or numbering. The substance of the protection (gross misdemeanor) is unchanged.
Core provisions confirmed from cached files: Section 609.2231 subd. 11 (transit operator assault/bodily fluid offense — gross misdemeanor) and 609.855 subd. 2 (interference) both verified. Minnesota's transit coverage is correctly described as strong.
Virginia Mostly Accurate
  • Minor 2025 amendment not noted (transit provision unchanged)
    TSV does not note any 2025 amendment.
    What the cached file shows: Va. Code § 18.2-57 was amended in 2025 (2025 c. 361). The transit operator protection in subsection (F) was not changed by this amendment, but for currency the note should reference the 2025 act as the most recent version.

Wisconsin Mostly Accurate
  • Minor Old section 940.20 citation — should no longer be used
    TSV notes: "older sources may cite section 940.20."
    What the cached file confirms: 2025 Wisconsin Act 24 comprehensively reorganized Wisconsin's battery statutes. The transit-vehicle operator protection is now exclusively in § 940.62(1)(e). Section 940.20 should not be cited for this provision. The TSV's caution about 940.20 is useful but could be stated more definitively.
Fully verified from cached file (wi_940_62.html): 940.62(1)(e) covers operators, drivers, or passengers of public transit vehicles (Class I felony — up to 3.5 years/$10,000). Three-part coverage (while on vehicle, after being forced off, while prevented from accessing) confirmed. 940.51(19) "public transit vehicle" definition confirmed. 2025 Act 24 confirmed as the source.
